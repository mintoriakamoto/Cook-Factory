# Brainstorm: the wetlease novel (untitled cyberpunk)

**Date:** 2026-07-23

## Premise

Isla Marrec is a wetlease courier in a port megacity where the wealthy rent
storage in the hippocampi of the poor. She carries strangers' memories for pay
and is saving to buy back her mother's confiscated memories from the Ledger's
orphan-estate auctions. Among her hosted files she finds something that should
not exist: a fully notarized recording of her own murder — provenance chain
intact, timestamped three weeks from now. She cannot sell it, cannot delete it,
and cannot stop dreaming it. The stake is doubled: the recording is the only
asset valuable enough to trade for her mother's life, and hosting it makes her
both the witness and the evidence in a killing that has not happened yet.

## World

Memory here is property, and property means papers. Copies of a memory are
trivial to make and worthless to hold: value lives in the provenance chain
kept by the Ledger, the notary-bank that records every transfer of custody,
and a copy reads as forgery the moment anyone checks it — the papers are worth
money, not the paint. Hosting has a cost the brochures omit: the render-tax.
Hosts dream the lives they carry, and long-term hosts slowly lose the
provenance of their own past; Isla has already lost her mother's face, which
is why the buy-back is a race against her own decay. Below the seawall lies
the Estuary, the drowned old town where unlicensed hosts live on tide-gated
streets that only connect at low water. Estuary hosts ink provenance tattoos,
the chain-of-custody of what they carry written on skin, and keep the Drowned
Registry: a heretical, witness-based answer to the Ledger where memory is
vouched by people instead of papers. The hard edge of the world: nothing
outside the Ledger can verify a forgery. The Drowned Registry can vouch, but
it cannot prove, and that asymmetry is what keeps the Ledger's monopoly and
the whole city's economy standing.

## Cast

- **Isla Marrec**: wetlease courier and involuntary host of her own murder.
  Wants to trade the recording back to the man it incriminates in exchange
  for her mother's memories. Collides with everyone: her trade is Pryce's
  exposure and Nef's wasted proof.
- **Registrar Halloway Pryce**: head of the Ledger's orphan-estate division,
  quietly auctioning the memories of the unclaimed dead. The murder recording
  notarizes a forgery he signed. Wants it erased, and its host with it if the
  price is right. Collides with Nef, who needs the file intact, and with
  Isla, who needs it valuable.
- **Brother Nef**: a forgetting priest of the Estuary who performs illegal
  deletions as sacrament. Wants the recording preserved and delivered as
  living proof that the Ledger can be forged — a heresy worth more to the
  Estuary than any single life, including Isla's. Collides with Pryce on the
  file's survival and with Isla on what her own murder is for.

## Tone

Salt-rot noir. The glamour tech lives uptown behind the seawall and the story
never goes there for long; it lives at the waterline, where the tide decides
which streets exist and everything metal is bleeding orange. Sentences carry
wet weight, dialogue is bartering even when it is tender, and the future feels
secondhand — every miracle in the book has already been pawned twice. A
stranger writing a scene from this page should reach for low light, standing
water, and a negotiation that is really a confession.

## Threads

- **The provenance chain**: links the murder file to Pryce's signature. Every
  custody stamp on the recording is a scene. Pays off at the Ledger reveal,
  when the chain shows the forgery was notarized in-house.
- **The buy-back**: links Isla's mother to the render-tax. The price of the
  confiscated memories rises while Isla's memory of the woman decays, and the
  first thing she lost was the face. Pays off when she must choose which past
  survives: her mother's archive or her own remaining provenance.
- **The sacrament**: links Brother Nef's forgetting rite to the finale. What
  begins as the mercy he offers strangers becomes the question the whole book
  asks of the recording itself. Pays off in the last act: deletion as mercy
  against testimony as heresy, and Isla holding the only file that can be
  either.
- **The countdown**: links the timestamp to the pacing. Three weeks out, and
  whether that date is exact or corrupt decides whether this is a fate story
  or a conspiracy story; the reveal must recolor every chapter behind it.

## Open Questions

- Is the recording real or a forgery? Answered by deciding which ending Isla
  earns, not which mechanism is cleverer: forgery makes it a conspiracy story
  and Pryce load-bearing; real makes it a fate story and hands the finale to
  Nef's sacrament. The author holds this one.
- POV and tense: first person present was floated and deliberately demoted at
  recap from decided to open. Answered by drafting the cold open both ways
  and keeping the one that survives a read-aloud.
- The title. Answered late, after the draft knows what it is about.
- Is the timestamp exact by design or corrupt by design? Answered together
  with the real-vs-forgery question; the pair must be decided before chapter
  outlining because the countdown paces the whole middle.
